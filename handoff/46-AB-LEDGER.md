## 4. Method

### 4.1 Why not just difference the frames
Between two launches the PT sample sequence, the denoiser's temporal history
and the background NPCs all differ. On S2, measured:

| measured over | dLum (E1−E0) | sd |
|---|---|---|
| raw rectangle over the face | −0.14 | **6.10** |
| segmented skin | +0.07 | 1.07 |
| ctrl ceiling | −0.03 | 6.08 |
| ctrl wall L | −0.06 | 7.55 |
| ctrl floor R | −0.12 | 1.01 |

A rectangle's mean is dominated by hair strands, silhouette edges and
background. **Any skin delta below ~0.3/255 is inside the spread of the
non-skin controls** and means nothing. Masks are built from the **baseline**
frame only, so they cannot be biased by the effect being measured.

### 4.2 What the metrics are for
- **Mean dLum**, reported as a **% of the region mean** — skin sits at lum ~95
  (S1), ~31 (S2), ~60 (S3), so /255 numbers are not comparable across scenes.
- **Grazing bins** (distance to the mask silhouette). Every skin term here
  (`c1` Fresnel, `dcouple`, `micro_k`) is a function of NoV/NoL and must peak
  at the edge. A flat or reversed gradient means the term is not reaching those
  pixels, whatever the mean says. This is the test that found §6.1.
- **Texture energy** (`ab_texture.py`) — high-pass std inside the mask. Added
  after the user's verdict on `rough-1.3` (§5.2) could not be explained by any
  mean. This turned out to be the metric that matches perception.
- Alignment is checked every run. Photo mode reproduces the camera exactly:
  every pair phase-correlates to `0,0` with peak/sd 190–1240.

### 4.3 KNOWN DEFECT: `--tone` bins are biased, and are kept that way

`ab_compare.py --tone` assigns pixels to bins by the **baseline** image's own
luminance and then reports (test − baseline) per bin. The baseline's per-pixel
noise therefore drives bin assignment and the difference is anti-correlated
with it: pixels that landed in a low bin partly *because* they were noisily
dark show a spuriously positive delta, and the top bin a spuriously negative
one. **Regression to the mean.** Binning by the test image flips the artefact.

This has NOT been fixed, deliberately — the `--tone` numbers quoted in §5 are
what the shipped script produces, and a reviewer must be able to reproduce
them. `dev/ab_bias_check.py` quantifies the bias instead, by re-binning on a
smoothed baseline (tone is low-frequency, PT noise is high-frequency, so
smoothing decorrelates assignment from noise without destroying the lit/shadow
structure). Where the smoothed rows agree with the raw row, the finding is
real. Where they do not, the raw row is an artefact. Results, S1, 6 bins:

| binning | q1 | q2 | q3 | q4 | q5 | q6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E1−E0**, raw baseline | +1.32% | +0.82% | −0.02% | −0.34% | +0.62% | +0.78% |
| E1−E0, raw test (flipped) | −0.69% | +0.04% | +0.35% | +0.58% | +0.86% | +1.12% |
| E1−E0, smoothed σ=8 | +0.26% | +0.24% | +0.05% | +0.30% | +0.71% | +1.06% |
| E1−E0, smoothed σ=32 | +0.16% | +0.29% | −0.02% | +0.13% | +1.00% | +1.02% |
| **E2a−E1**, raw baseline | +2.17% | +1.71% | +1.48% | +1.70% | +1.71% | +0.44% |
| E2a−E1, smoothed σ=8 | +2.01% | +1.65% | +1.65% | +1.62% | +1.77% | +0.45% |
| E2a−E1, smoothed σ=32 | +1.93% | +1.51% | +1.75% | +1.91% | +1.63% | +0.39% |
| **E2b−E1**, raw baseline | +2.07% | +1.64% | +1.45% | +1.60% | +1.28% | +0.79% |
| E2b−E1, smoothed σ=8 | +1.89% | +1.55% | +1.61% | +1.56% | +1.34% | +0.80% |

So: **the E2a/E2b tone shapes survive** — stable to within ~0.2 pp across all
σ, including the characteristic drop in the top bin. **The E1−E0 shape does
not.** An earlier draft of this document claimed E1 "darkens shadowed skin
−1.05% and brightens lit skin +1.20%, increasing facial contrast". That was the
artefact. Unbiased, E1 is **+0.2…+0.3% on dark skin rising to ~+1.0% on bright
skin** — a mild contrast increase with no darkening anywhere. §6.2 is corrected
accordingly.

### 4.4 Other things wrong with this method
- **No measured noise floor.** No two launches share a config *and* a scene, so
  the floor is inferred from non-skin controls, not measured. Any future result
  in the 0.1–0.5/255 range needs a same-config repeat before it is believed.
- **E1 is not a single variable.** It enables `skin`, `shadowcull`, `ptq`,
  `ptrefl`, `ptmsggx` together. Every "E1" number is the whole stack. A
  `tier=1 skin=off` launch isolates the skin BRDF and has not been run.
- **n is not independent.** ~90k pixels, but the denoiser correlates neighbours
  heavily, so the effective sample size is far smaller than n and no p-value is
  quoted anywhere. Control regions are the null, not a t-test.
- **Session drift is unmodelled.** The ~+1.5% mid-tone lift common to *both*
  E2 rungs (§5.1) is unattributed — the controls do not support calling it
  drift (§9 c3) and no same-config repeat exists to call it real. Only the
  *difference between* the two rungs is safe.
- **One character, one save, one time of day.** Nothing here generalises to
  other skin tones; `micro_k` in particular scales with (1 − luminance) and this
  character is pale.

## 6. Findings

### 6.1 Tier-1 `c1` does not reach bounce-lit skin — `42` reopens
**Confidence: high** *(for the S2 null; the S1 half was overstated — §9 c1)*.
S1's actual grazing profile is +0.461/+0.541/+0.582/**+0.731**/+0.502/+0.396 —
a shallow hump peaking at 14–22 px, **not** an edge-max; weak positive
evidence only. The strong half is S2 (bounce-lit): the signature is
**reversed** and monotonic over six bins with flat variance, −0.149 at the
silhouette rising to +0.151 face-on, where the retro term alone predicts
≈ +1.3/255 at the edge (§9 c1). It survives eroding the mask 4 px further
(−0.068 → +0.155), so it is not a silhouette artefact — and no
diffuse-Fresnel term can produce a reversed edge profile.
The S1 difference heatmap shows coherent structure following the pore/freckle
texture; the S2 heatmap's face is flat black with every bright edge in the
background.

This is `45` §4's *"S2 == E0 while S1 differs"* row, but the question is
sharper than `42` framed it: **the modules were served** — 77/77 unique `.dxil`
HITs on both E1 launches — so the splice is present but its effect is not.
§9 c2 claimed the two phi-lifted modules are `99bb7c26` **and** `ab0bc2fe` —
"the two GI resolvers" — so "the lift missed the dispatching module" is
excluded. **§12 corrects this: `ab0bc2fe` writes an integer index buffer, not
radiance. Only `99bb7c26` can put c1 on a pixel, and the exclusion does not
hold.** Three
hypotheses remain: (a) the guard phi's class-0 path is what these pixels
actually take (fetch skipped ⇒ gate reads 0 — `42`'s own "safe to gate on"
note describes exactly this case); (b) this scene's GI resolve happens in a
module outside the 77 anchored ones; (c) the term is present but attenuated
below the floor by the denoiser. One `skinspec=probe-cls` launch in the S2
interior separates them (§9.4 L2).
*Falsified by:* a launch where S2's grazing gradient turns edge-positive.
*Caveat:* the S2 pair is the **old character** (both frames). Not yet repeated.

### 6.2 The shipping default's skin effect is near-threshold — and it costs detail
> **The "costs detail" half is DEAD (§11.2)** — −15.9% is inside a +58.5%
> floor. The `ptclamp` hypothesis it motivated has no observation left to
> explain, and L3 is dropped (§11.6). The near-threshold half is untouched.

**Confidence: medium-high.** E1 vs E0 on S1 is +0.52% in the mean and, binned
without the §4.3 bias, +0.2…+0.3% on dark skin rising to ~+1.0% on bright skin.
That is at or below what a viewer resolves. `CURRENT.md` lists tier-1 `c1`

Worse, E1 has **−15.9% fine texture energy vs bit-exact vanilla** (§5.2). The

*Prime suspect:* `ptclamp`, the firefly clamp — bright outliers on skin **are**
the pore and freckle speculars. Untested; needs one `tier=1 ptclamp=off`
launch. `ptbounce`/`ptrefl`/`ptmsggx` and the `c1` term itself are alternatives
and are not separable without further single-variable launches.
*Confounded by:* §4.4 — E1 is five features at once.

### 6.3 The roughness axis works, and it is a texture effect
**Confidence: high**, and the only finding with independent visual
confirmation. The α rewrite (`44` §3.1) is hitting the right ids: the two

**Direction not yet chosen** — the user deferred, correctly: it is an aesthetic

highlights tip toward the oily/plastic look `33` §2's ladder got wrong; the
risk on `rough-1.3` is leaving detail on the table.
*Open:* both cost detail in dim light (§5.3), which neither `44` nor `45`
predicted and which no rung addresses.

## 12. `ab0bc2fe` is not a GI resolver — §9 c2 mis-identified it

Found while pre-flighting L2, before the launch. `skin.set/probe-cls` has
**76** modules where every skin rung has 77. The one it declines is
`ab0bc2fee876d489` — which §9 c2 named as one of "the two GI resolvers" and
used to *exclude* the "lift missed the module" hypothesis.

### 12.1 What was measured (static, no launch needed)

Reproduce: `spirv-dis ~/callisto_dump/ab0bc2fee876d489.dxil.spv` and
`python3 dev/patch_subtype_probe.py <asm> --tier cls --outdir /tmp/x`
→ `error: no image write reachable for the hunt`.

| fact | `ab0bc2fe` | `99bb7c26` (the other) |
|---|---|---|
| sole `OpImageWrite` target | `OpTypeImage %int 2D … 2` (int storage) | float 2D |
| value written | `OpCompositeConstruct %v4uint` → `OpBitcast %v4int` | `OpCompositeConstruct %v4float` |
| components | `(a, b, a, a)`, each `OpSelect(valid, RoundEven→ConvertFToS(f), 0xFFFFFFFF)` | radiance floats |
| guards on those values | `OpIsInf`/`OpIsNan` on the float, `OpULessThan … %uint_2`, a `&255`+1 byte counter | none |
| workgroup / subgroup use | LDS, `%SubgroupSize`, `LocalSize 32 16 1` | none, `LocalSize` plain |
| in `swaps.skin` (tier-1 c1 installed) | **yes** | yes |
| paintable by `probe-cls` | **no** | yes |

Census of the anchored set: **76 of 77 write `v4float`; exactly one writes
`v4uint` — this module.**

### 12.2 What it means

A rounded integer with an "invalid" sentinel, bounds-checked, carrying an
age/count byte, written to an int storage image at a pixel coordinate, is a
**sample-index / reservoir buffer** — a ReSTIR-style "which sample do I reuse
here" pass. It evaluates the full material stack (which is why the 1/pi +
0.107508637 anchor matched it) in order to compute a reuse *weight*, and then
writes **indices, not colour**.

So:

- **§9 c2 is corrected.** There is one GI *resolver* among the anchored
  modules that can put c1 on a pixel, not two. "The lift reached both
  resolvers" is not the reassurance it was read as.
- The tier-1 `c1` splice **is** installed in `ab0bc2fe` (it is in
  `swaps.skin`), where it can only perturb sample-reuse weighting. It cannot
  brighten a pixel directly. Whether that is harmless or a subtle bias on
  which samples survive is untested and not currently worth a launch.
- **§6.1's hypothesis (b) is stronger than §9 left it.** For c1 to reach
  bounce-lit skin, `99bb7c26` (or another of the 76 colour-writers) has to be
  what shades those pixels.

### 12.3 What it does to L2 — run it, the reading is *cleaner*

The instinct on finding the gap was that L2 is now a false-negative trap:
the probe cannot paint `ab0bc2fe`, so a red-less face would be ambiguous.
It is not, because `ab0bc2fe` **cannot paint by any mechanism** — it has no
colour output to tint. It is not a candidate explanation for an unpainted
face. L2 reads:

| S2 interior under `skinspec=probe-cls` | conclusion |
|---|---|
| face goes **red** (class 1 = red, `HUNT_PALETTE`) | the class gate passes in a colour-writing anchored module ⇒ §6.1 hypothesis (c), term present but suppressed below the floor. `42` closes. |
| face **not** red | the pixels are not shaded by any of the 76 colour-writers with class 1 ⇒ (a) gate reads something else, or (b) an unanchored module. Then `probe-sub` per §9.4. (b) is now the favourite. |

Caveat unchanged from §9.4: the paint sites are the radiance writes, not the
c1 lines, but they hang off the same class anchor.

Standing lesson for `GOTCHAS`: **"the module carries the patch" is not
"the patch can reach a pixel."** Check what the module *writes* before
building an argument on its coverage. A module can carry the whole BRDF and
emit nothing but indices.

### 15.2 What this actually settles

- **`ptclamp` is exonerated for the detail loss.** Three mod launches with the
  clamp in two different states put S3's scene-wide fine energy at −9.6%,
  −9.9% and −9.2% against vanilla. The clamp moves it by 0.4 pp. §6.2's
  mechanism — "the firefly clamp eats pore speculars" — is **wrong**.
- **`ptclamp` is convicted of dimming dim-lit skin.** Small, real, and
  arguably the clamp doing its job; whether it is worth the brightness is a
  taste call, not a bug.
- **The −10% scene-wide fine-energy loss in dim light is unexplained and very
  stable** (three configs, 0.7 pp spread). It is scene-wide, so it is not the
  skin overlay and not the SSS kernel. Remaining: `ptreg`, `ptbounce`,
  `ptrefl`, `ptmsggx`, `shadowcull`.

### 15.3 Next: `ptreg`

Path regularization is *designed* to trade high-frequency variance for
smoothness — it widens lobes on later bounces specifically to kill noise. A
stable, scene-wide, dim-light-only loss of fine energy is what it should look
like. It is the strongest remaining candidate by mechanism, not just by
elimination.

**L6 (`ptreg=off`, clamp restored, S1 + S3) predicts:** S3 non-skin fine
energy moves substantially toward vanilla's 2.689 from ~2.43. If it does not,
the cause is `ptbounce`/`ptrefl`/`ptmsggx`/`shadowcull` and the elimination
continues.

A useful side-effect either way: L6 is a fourth mod-config sample against the
single vanilla capture. If some config finally matches vanilla's non-skin
2.689, that also retires the worry that L5 is simply an anomalous baseline —
which one sample cannot currently rule out.

Not `ptclamp` (§15), not `ptreg` (§16) — each moves it ~1 pp. The enabled

### 17.3 Caveat that will matter when the culprit is found

(`46` §9.3 raised this for the skin rungs; it applies here too). If
`ptbounce` is the cause, the honest description may be **"the mod converges
dim-light GI better, so there is less residual sampling noise"** — an
improvement that this metric scores as a loss. The number localises the
mechanism; only the user's eye decides whether the dim scene looks better or
worse with it. Get that verdict at the same launch that identifies the
switch: shoot S3 with the switch off, and look at the two frames side by side
before writing a conclusion.

### 18.3 GOTCHA: the journal's `payload=` is a stat hash, not a content hash

`sync_settings.sh:339` computes it from `stat -c '%n %s %Y'` — name, size,
mtime. `ptq/rcm` and `ptq/rcbm` are different builds (`6fe05ecc3ab345df` vs
`55ed4e5c6884ab71`) whose files share names, sizes and build-run mtimes, so
**both launches logged `payload=225acb871d94a4b8`.** Equal payload does **not**
prove equal bytes served.

Cache eviction is unaffected — it keys off the whole `want` string, which
carries `ptq=$ptq_state` — and `cache=cleared` did fire. But when a launch
must be proven to have served something different, hash the overlay:

```bash
cat ~/.local/lib/callisto/swaps.ptq/*.spv | sha256sum | cut -c1-16
```

Two launches were nearly discarded as "served the same thing" on the strength
of a colliding payload field.
